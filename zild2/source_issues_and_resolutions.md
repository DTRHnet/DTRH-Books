# Source Issues and Resolutions

This log records problems identified in the draft and the editorial decisions used to repair them. It is intentionally separate from the reader-facing rewrite.

## Global issues

| Source issue | Why it weakens cohesion | Rewrite decision | Affected chapters |
|---|---|---|---|
| The manuscript alternates between narrative prose, field notes, journal inserts, confessional logs, sensory snapshots, and process captions without a stable hierarchy. | The reader cannot always tell whether a device is part of the story or a drafting instruction. | Retain only forms that create character or dramatic distance; integrate the rest into normal prose and remove labels such as “Planting Seed.” | 1–17 |
| Several chapter titles are truncated or grammatically inconsistent in the EPUB extraction and table of contents. | It makes the novel appear unfinished and obscures chapter identity. | Use the canonical title map in the continuity bible and apply consistent title punctuation. | 1–17 |
| Chapter 15 appears twice in the source EPUB as two consecutive segments. | It suggests either an accidental duplicate or an unmarked continuation. | Treat both segments as one Chapter 15 in the rewrite and document the boundary only in editorial notes. | 15 |
| The manuscript repeatedly explains the same motifs—breath, pulse, mycelium, memory, and hidden networks—without always advancing their meaning. | Repetition can flatten escalation and make foreshadowing feel announced. | Keep the strongest recurrence and make each later appearance change the motif’s significance. | 1–17 |
| The source uses highly specific scientific or technical claims as atmosphere without always establishing their limits. | Unbounded systems can feel arbitrary when they later drive plot. | Preserve speculative texture but state operational consequences clearly when a system affects a character’s choice. | 1–17 |

## Chapter-specific issues

| Chapter | Source issue to investigate | Planned resolution |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Lauren’s surname and the LUCIFERIN payload are introduced with ambiguity; several labeled inserts interrupt the opening. | Choose one Lauren surname after checking later usage; turn the payload into a quiet, character-relevant anomaly rather than a labeled seed. |
| 2 | Transit and launch chronology may be compressed; technical exposition and crew introduction compete for attention. | Establish the sequence of launch, cryo, navigation, and first anomaly through scene action. |
| 3 | The Ark’s duct-beat, crew fatigue, and interpersonal tension recur in similar forms. | Give each recurrence a new consequence and clarify who notices what. |
| 4 | The chapter contains an epigraph-like sentence attached to the heading and may shift from transit to memory without a clear hinge. | Separate epigraph from scene only if it has thematic value; use a sensory or emotional transition for the shift. |
| 5 | The chapter’s source title is split across EPUB paragraphs; plant and Ark imagery sometimes repeats without causal development. | Normalize title and make botanical observations drive an investigation or decision. |
| 6 | Groundfall introduces the living forest with intense imagery but may understate immediate survival objectives. | Anchor ecological wonder in landing logistics, injury, supplies, and the crew’s first practical choice. |
| 7 | “Prime Pulse” material appears to combine discovery, technological explanation, and emotional reaction. | Stage the pulse as a sensory discovery first, then reveal its implications through character disagreement. |
| 8 | Inhibitors and the forest’s intelligence risk arriving as exposition rather than evidence. | Seed the inhibitor concept through damaged traces, behavior, and competing interpretations before naming it. |
| 9 | The return to the Ark may repeat earlier shipboard descriptions and shift location abruptly. | Use the return as a reversal: familiar Ark spaces now carry evidence of changed agency and threat. |
| 10 | Predator material and two-moon imagery can become episodic if not tied to a concrete objective. | Make the predator encounter force a choice about pursuit, rescue, or retreat and advance the larger mystery. |
| 11 | The Loom’s function and origin may be unclear before its awakening. | Define what the characters can observe, what they infer, and what remains unknown; avoid a single explanatory dump. |
| 12 | “Hollow Dawn” appears to rely on subterranean or atmospheric imagery whose geography needs checking. | Clarify physical movement and make the Hollow a place with rules rather than only a mood. |
| 13 | The forest’s agency may be described as both protective and predatory without a clear moral or ecological logic. | Preserve ambiguity while establishing consistent behavior: the forest responds to relationship, disturbance, and signal. |
| 14 | The Sundering escalates the Ark’s structural crisis rapidly; the source may not fully connect earlier anomalies to the breach. | Pay off duct-beat, LUCIFERIN, and system anomalies as converging causes rather than unrelated surprises. |
| 15 | The duplicate source segment and “last stand” framing create uncertainty about sequence and survivors. | Merge segments, clarify defensive positions and objectives, and make each loss alter the tactical situation. |
| 16 | “Ashes of the Ark” risks becoming aftermath summary rather than a scene of consequence. | Give the aftermath a physical setting, a survivor’s immediate task, and a decisive interpretation of what was lost. |
| 17 | The final chapter is longer and more reflective, with potential appendix-like or essayistic material. | Shape the ending around a specific act of remembering or transmitting, then allow the broader thematic resonance to emerge from it. |

## Resolution status

This log will be updated after every chapter. A source issue is not considered resolved until the chapter rewrite and the whole-manuscript pass agree that the decision works with later events.
