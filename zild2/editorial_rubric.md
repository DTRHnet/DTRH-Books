# Zild Rewrite Rubric and Editorial Control System

## Purpose

This rubric governs the rewrite of every chapter of **Zild**. The rewrite preserves the core narrative, characters, setting, and thematic direction while replacing draft-process language with finished prose and ensuring that every chapter belongs to the same novel.

Chapter One is the principal style reference. Its strongest qualities are atmospheric specificity, controlled sensory detail, humane intelligence, and a serious speculative register. The rewrite will retain those qualities while reducing repetition, clarifying chronology, and making the prose less visibly assembled from notes, inserts, and labeled devices.

## Chapter review table

Every chapter receives a review entry in the chapter notes. Each category is marked **meets**, **needs revision**, or **intentionally retained**, followed by a concise explanation for any material choice.

| Category | Standard |
|---|---|
| Voice | Prose is natural, intelligent, vivid, and consistent with the established literary-science-fiction voice. |
| Clarity | The reader can identify the viewpoint, setting, time, immediate objective, and consequence of the scene. |
| Character integrity | Names, relationships, appearance, expertise, knowledge, motivation, and emotional responses remain stable. |
| Point of view | Information is limited to what the viewpoint can perceive, infer, remember, or plausibly learn. |
| Scene purpose | Every scene changes knowledge, stakes, relationships, physical circumstances, or emotional direction. |
| Pacing | Description and reflection support movement; exposition is distributed rather than delivered in blocks. |
| Dialogue | Speech has distinct voices, subtext, and purposeful friction; it does not exist only to explain lore. |
| Worldbuilding | Technology, ecology, ship systems, terminology, and rules are introduced consistently and without unnecessary glossary prose. |
| Foreshadowing | Later turns receive subtle earlier preparation through image, behavior, object, system anomaly, or dialogue. |
| Causality | Major events arise from choices, established conditions, or intelligible pressures rather than coincidence. |
| Emotional continuity | Reactions follow from what characters have experienced and what they know at that point. |
| Ending | The chapter ends on a meaningful turn, discovery, decision, threat, or altered understanding. |
| Line editing | Grammar, punctuation, syntax, paragraphing, transitions, and word choice are clean and deliberate. |
| Draft-artifact removal | Labels such as “Planting Seed,” “Sensory Snapshot,” “Journal Insert,” or “Confessional Mode” are either integrated into prose or removed. |

## Rewrite rules

The finished chapters will not use process labels as if they were part of the novel’s architecture. A field note, journal entry, log, or memory may remain when its form is dramatically necessary, but it must be introduced naturally and formatted consistently. A label that merely announces authorial intent will disappear.

Foreshadowing must be legible in retrospect rather than conspicuous on first reading. A recurring duct-beat, an anomalous checksum, a warm unmarked cylinder, a pattern in the forest, or an inconsistency in Spacy’s behavior can carry future significance without being called a seed. Every deliberate setup will be tracked in the ledger, but the ledger will never be visible in reader-facing chapter prose.

When the source contradicts itself, the rewrite will choose the interpretation that preserves the most established story with the least retroactive damage. The choice will be logged, especially when it affects a name, timeline, relationship, technology rule, or later payoff.

## Continuity bible

The continuity bible will track the following stable facts and will be updated after each chapter:

| Domain | Items to track |
|---|---|
| Characters | Isaac Porter, Lauren Cho/Scott naming, Lena, Ava, Markus, Spacy, and every recurring human or nonhuman presence. |
| Relationships | Family histories, command relationships, trust, rivalry, affection, and changing alliances. |
| Chronology | Earth departure, Ark transit, planetary approach, groundfall, exploration, return, the Sundering, and aftermath. |
| Locations | Earth bunker, Ark decks and sectors, landing site, breathing forest, neon canopy, underground structures, and any named regions of Zild. |
| Systems | Cryostasis, Mirrorglass trajectory, duct-beat, LUCIFERIN, the Loom, inhibitors, Spacy’s architecture, and Ark integrity. |
| Ecology | Mycelium networks, bioluminescence, predators, spores, communicating organisms, and the distinction between living system and threat. |
| Motifs | Breath, roots, tunnels, pulse, ash, two moons, memory, entanglement, and the idea that the forest is relational rather than merely scenic. |
| Open threads | Questions that should remain active, receive an answer, or be deliberately reserved for a later volume. |

## Chapter note template

Each rewritten chapter will have a companion note in `chapter_notes/` containing the following sections:

### Dramatic function

A paragraph explaining what the chapter does in the novel and how its ending changes the reader’s understanding or the characters’ options.

### Major continuity decisions

A short table listing source inconsistency, rewrite decision, rationale, and affected chapters.

### Foreshadowing decisions

A short table listing the setup added, the scene where it appears, and the later chapter or thematic payoff it supports.

### Rubric assessment

A table marking each rubric category as **meets**, **needs revision**, or **intentionally retained**, with notes only where useful.

## Final acceptance test

The manuscript passes only when all chapters can be read in sequence without requiring the source draft to explain a name, timeline, motivation, or system rule; when process captions no longer appear in reader-facing prose; when important setups have plausible payoffs or documented intentional openness; and when the whole book sounds like one deliberate novel rather than a sequence of differently edited drafts.
